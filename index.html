Where the points are, and where the hours go

English is the cheapest points on the test. On Test 1's conversion table, raw 35 is a 29, 36 is a 31 and 37 is a 33. Two more right answers are worth 4 scale points. Four of your seven English misses were the same question family (the writer's goal: add, delete, or place a sentence), and two were No Change picks. Those are learnable rules, so English gets 8 hours of rule sessions plus two timed sets.

Math has the most raw room. You missed 10 scored questions, and every one is a specific, fixable concept from your list. On Test 1's table, raw 34 is a 31 and 36 is a 33. Math gets the largest share, 12 hours of topic sessions plus two timed sets, because that's where most of the missing questions are and it's the section you're least sure of. Four of eleven misses were stopping one step early, which costs nothing to fix: reread the question before you click.

Reading is near the ceiling. You missed 2 of 27 scored, with 8 minutes to spare. There's little to gain and one bad passage can cost 2 points (raw 24 is a 32), so Reading gets just enough to hold the 34: one session in week 4, one block in the taper week, and the three timed practice test sections.

The plan's arithmetic: English 33 + Math 31 + Reading 34 = 98, a 33. If Math reaches 32, English only needs 32.

Checkpoints and what to do with them

Each practice test is split across three weekdays, one section per day in one timed sitting, because your weekends are off. You lose the stamina of a full sitting, so the taper week includes one longer session. Score each test with its own scoring key, counting only scored questions.

  • On track: hit the composite target, or miss it by 1 with Math or English up from the last test.
  • Off track at Checkpoint 1 (under 31, or Math raw not above 31): drop the second English session in weeks 4 and 5 and give it to Math.
  • Off track at Checkpoint 3 (under 32): you can still test on Dec 12 and keep it as a first sitting. The next national date is Feb 27, 2027 (regular registration deadline Jan 22). A test date change costs $49. This is only an option. You said you have no backup date.

    Session rules

    • Run a timer for every block. Timed blocks mean real test pace (see Pacing targets).
    • Keep an error log (a note or a sheet): date, source, section, topic, why you missed it, and the fix. Use four causes: concept, careless, stopped early, and timing. Warm-ups run off this log.
    • Review your guesses the same way as your misses.
    • Keep Practice Tests 2, 3 and 4 unseen until their checkpoint days. Their questions are the only official full tests you have left.
    • Check a block off only when you've finished it. If you miss a day, make it up and check it off from Catch up on the Today tab.

    Test rules worth knowing

    Verified on act.org on Oct 9, 2026. Sources are linked on each line.

    • The enhanced ACT is English (50 questions, 35 min), Math (45 questions, 50 min) and Reading (36 questions, 40 min). Science (40 questions, 40 min) and Writing are optional add-ons. ACT enhancements
    • Each section includes unscored field-test questions, and they aren't labeled: English has 40 scored, Math 41, and Reading 27. Preparing for the ACT, p. 3
    • The composite is the average of the English, Math and Reading scale scores (each 1 to 36), rounded to the nearest whole number with halves rounding up. Science doesn't count toward it. Skipping Science costs you nothing on the composite. Scoring key, p. 9 · enhancements
    • Math questions have 4 answer choices. There's no penalty for guessing, so answer every question. ACT Industry Insights · Preparing for the ACT, p. 4
    • Calculators are allowed only in Math. The online test has the Desmos graphing calculator built in. Paper testers bring their own permitted calculator. Calculator policy
    • Superscore: English + Math + Reading, using your best score in each across test dates, divided by 3 and rounded. ACT calculates it automatically once you've tested more than once, and you request superscore reports from MyACT. Whether a school or employer accepts a superscore is up to them. Superscore FAQs · Sending scores
    • Fees: $70 for the base test, $5 for the Science add-on, $25 for Writing, $42 for late registration, $49 to change dates, and $20 for each additional score report. Registration includes reports to you, your high school, and up to 4 colleges if you list codes when you register. ACT fees
    • Unverified The ACT sending-scores page also shows an unlabeled $32 fee. The fees page says $20 per additional report.
    • Unverified How McKinsey and BCG treat an ACT superscore versus a single sitting. I found no official statement. Ask a recruiter or your career center, and plan on one strong single sitting.
    • Check yourself Whether your Dec 12 registration is online or paper, and whether you added Science. If you registered for Science, you can remove it through the late deadline (Nov 29). Online scores post Dec 17 and paper scores Dec 22.

    Reteach a math topic
